# SOC Incident Report – Simulated Multi-Stage Attack Scenario

> **About this report.** It is based on **synthetic lab logs** (`data/security_logs.csv`, 455 events, 20 Oct 2026 09:00:13–09:59:32), not on a real organisation.
> The first draft was generated by the Report Writer agent in the notebook run of 20 Oct 2026. It was then **manually edited** to remove claims the logs do not support (see the change log at the end).
> **All response actions are recommendations; no action was taken.**

## 1. Executive Summary
In one hour of simulated logs, the rule engine flagged seven kinds of suspicious activity: a phishing-site visit, an SSH brute-force attempt, regular outbound "beacon-like" connections, a port scan, SQL-injection attempts, a privilege escalation and large outbound transfers.

The most serious indicators are:
- a successful `admin` login after 45 failed attempts from the same external IP;
- 30 outbound connections at ~60-second intervals from host `10.0.0.23`, followed by about 2.0 GB of outbound transfers from that host to the same external IP;
- a log entry showing user `intern01` obtaining root.

In the scenario, these **indicate possible** account and host compromise. They have not been verified, and the logs do not show what data was transferred. The recommended actions below are prioritised by severity.

## 4. Assessment – Facts vs. Hypotheses

**Supported by the logs**
- INC-03 and INC-07 share both the source host (10.0.0.23) and the destination (45.33.32.156). They are probably one incident in the scenario: possible C2 activity followed by possible data exfiltration.
- The `admin` account had a successful login immediately after a brute-force burst from the same IP.
- The privilege-change log for `intern01` records a successful escalation to root.

**Not shown by the logs (hypotheses only)**
- That all seven activities belong to one attacker or campaign. The source IPs and accounts differ, and nothing links the phishing visit to the brute force.
- Whether `meera` submitted valid credentials. The POST shows that a form was submitted, not what it contained.
- What the attacker did after the `admin` login, or whether the SQL injection succeeded. An HTTP 200 response does not confirm exploitation.
- How 10.0.0.23 became infected, whether persistence exists, and what `archive.zip` contained, including whether it was sensitive.

## 5. Recommended Response (not executed)

**CRITICAL – response target 15 minutes**
1. **Host 10.0.0.23.** Isolate it via EDR/NAC while keeping it powered on for forensics. Block 45.33.32.156 at the perimeter. Use proxy, DLP and file-server logs to determine what was transferred.
2. **Account `admin` on 10.0.0.5.** Block 185.220.101.45, lock the account and end its sessions. Review the commands run after 09:12:17, then reset credentials and rotate SSH keys.
3. **Host 10.0.0.31 / user `intern01`.** Disable the account and isolate the host. Check for persistence (new users, cron jobs, SSH keys), then patch CVE-2021-4034 (polkit `pkexec`).

**HIGH – response target 1 hour**

4. **User `meera`.** Verify whether credentials were entered, then reset the password and revoke sessions. Block `micros0ft-login-verify.com` in DNS and the email gateway, and search mailboxes for the lure.
5. **Web server 10.0.0.80.** Block 91.240.118.22 and enable WAF SQL-injection rules. Check database logs for unexpected queries, and confirm that the affected endpoints use parameterised queries.

**MEDIUM – response target 24 hours**

6. **Port scan.** Block or rate-limit 203.0.113.77. Confirm that only required ports are exposed on 10.0.0.80.

---

## 6. Long-term Recommendations
1. Require phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2/WebAuthn), especially for privileged and SSH access.
2. Restrict SSH to a VPN or bastion host and add an account-lockout policy.
3. Apply egress filtering so outbound traffic is limited to approved ports and destinations.
4. Automate OS patching for local privilege-escalation flaws.
5. Deploy DLP monitoring for large outbound transfers.
6. Put a WAF in front of public web applications and add input validation in code.

---

## 7. Overall Risk Rating
**CRITICAL within the simulated scenario.** This rating follows from the rule-based severities (possible host compromise with large outbound transfers, a successful login after brute force, root escalation) and depends on the verification steps above.
